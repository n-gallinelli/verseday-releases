The problem

Most days end without a verdict.

You start with a plan. Then a meeting moves, something urgent lands, and by six o'clock the plan is a document you no longer recognise. You were busy. You can't say what you finished.

VerseDay times the work itself — a running clock on the task in front of you, not a guess you reconstruct at the end of the week. When you close the day, you see both numbers side by side.

Wednesday you planned six hours of deep work and got two and a half. That's not a failure. It's the first useful thing you've learned about your Wednesdays.

How it works

Three moments in a working day.

Morning

Plan the day

Pull in today's calendar, add what actually matters, and give each task an estimate. Group work under objectives so you can see which ones you're genuinely moving.

Midday

Time the work

Start a task and a small timer floats above whatever you're doing. Take a break and it waits. Nothing to remember, nothing to reconstruct — the clock is running on the real thing.

Evening

Close it out

A short shutdown: what you finished, what moved, where the hours went. The day ends deliberately instead of trailing off, and tomorrow starts from something true.

Why I built it

I kept finishing weeks I couldn't account for.

I'd plan a day properly, work hard through it, and still get to Friday unable to say where the time had gone. Every tool I tried wanted me to estimate in advance and report afterwards — two guesses, neither of them the truth.

So I built the thing I wanted: something that plans the day, runs a real clock on the work, and makes me close the day out before I walk away from it. It's the app I open first thing and last thing, every working day.

It's free because it was never meant to be a business. It runs entirely on your Mac because that's where I wanted my own data to stay.

— Nick

Your data

It stays on your Mac.

  • No account and no sign-in. You download it and open it.
  • No cloud and no sync. Everything lives in a single database file on your own machine, which you can export or back up whenever you like.
  • Nothing is sold, shared or analysed. There is no analytics service, no crash reporter and no tracking of any kind.
  • VerseDay checks for a new version automatically. That check sends no version number, no platform, no identifier and no personal data. The request reaches GitHub, which records an IP address and a browser identifier the way any web request does. This check can't currently be switched off.
  • Updates are cryptographically signed and the app refuses anything that isn't signed with the right key.

Calendar access is optional and only used to read today's events onto your plan. VerseDay never writes to your calendar.
